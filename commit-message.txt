docs(marketplace): add investor funding pitch with NEAR blockchain strategy

Adds a comprehensive investor-facing funding pitch document for the
Marketplace module, including a rigorous justification for choosing
NEAR Protocol over ERC-1155/Ethereum and full project management
operating model.

## New Documentation

### Marketplace Pitch (`docs/MARKETPLACE_PITCH.md`)
- **571-line** investor funding proposal for a $2M seed round
- Structured as a complete pitch deck in markdown — problem,
  solution, product demo, technology, market, traction, financials

### Key Sections

**1. Problem Statement**
- $2B voluntary carbon market with fragmented registries
- Opaque pricing, broken trust, unverifiable retirement
- Small participants locked out by lot sizes and broker fees

**2. Solution & Competitive Analysis**
- End-to-end pipeline: Measurement → Verification → Tokenization
  → Trading → Retirement (5 stages; competitors cover 1-2)
- Comparison table vs Toucan, KlimaDAO, CBL/Xpansiv, Flowcarbon,
  Carbonplace — DMRV is the only platform covering all 5 stages

**3. Product Demo (What's Built)**
- All 7 marketplace modules described: Browse, Trading Desk,
  Portfolio, Retirement & Impact, Analytics, NEAR Explorer,
  Registry Hub
- Emphasizes this is production-grade code, not mockups

**4. Why NEAR Protocol — And Not Ethereum/ERC-1155**
This is the centerpiece addition. Addresses the #1 investor question
head-on with 5 structural reasons:

  1. **Human-readable identity** — Retirement certificates show
     `acme-corp.near` instead of `0x2b8c...`; audit-grade prose
     vs machine logs for compliance officers

  2. **Rich on-chain metadata at viable cost** — MRV hash, registry
     serial, methodology, tonnage stored directly on-chain for ~$0.03;
     ERC-1155 pushes metadata off-chain to IPFS, weakening the
     chain-of-custody guarantee

  3. **Multi-tenant sub-accounts** — NEAR's `tenant.dmrv.near`
     hierarchy mirrors the PostgreSQL multi-tenant schema with
     protocol-level access control via function-call access keys

  4. **Predictable gas fees** — Trading Desk shows live fee estimates;
     NEAR gas doesn't spike with congestion like Ethereum L2s

  5. **No bridge risk** — Retirement (permanent burn) happens on a
     sharded L1 with no L2 bridge dependency; cites Ronin ($625M),
     Wormhole ($320M), Nomad ($190M) exploits

- Acknowledges NEAR's smaller ecosystem but frames it as advantage:
  < 5 carbon competitors on NEAR vs 50+ on Polygon; NEAR Foundation
  concentrated grant support; category leadership opportunity
- Confirms multi-chain expansion path: ERC-1155 on Polygon planned
  for Phase 2 (Month 18) with no frontend rewrite needed

**5. Competitive Landscape (Enhanced)**
- Added specific competitor failures: Flowcarbon raised $70M and
  paused token launch; KlimaDAO token lost 98%; Toucan faced
  whitelisting exploits
- Positions DMRV as a different infrastructure approach, not
  just another "bridge credits to Polygon" project

**6. Market Opportunity & Revenue Model**
- TAM: $50B by 2030 (McKinsey, BloombergNEF)
- SAM: ~$1B today, growing to ~$10B by 2030
- 5 revenue streams projecting ~$450K Year 1, ~$3-5M Year 2

**7. Use of Funds ($2M Seed Round)**
- Detailed budget: 63% engineering, 15% product/design, 2.5%
  security audits, 12.7% contingency
- 10 milestones from Month 3 (Backend API) to Month 18
  (EVM expansion + break-even)

**8. How We Work: Process, Requirements & Team Operations**
New section covering operational maturity:

- **Requirements Engineering** — References existing SRS (40+ user
  stories), data schema (43 tables), API specs (23 endpoints);
  describes feature pipeline for post-launch work
- **Project Management** — Agile/Scrum, 2-week sprints, 12-13
  sprints to launch, ceremony schedule (standups, planning,
  reviews, retros, grooming, architecture reviews)
- **Working Hours** — Core hours 10AM-4PM, flex around that,
  40-hour weeks, remote-first, no-meeting days Mon/Wed,
  async-first communication (Slack, Linear, GitHub, Loom, Notion)
- **Reporting Cadence** — Sprint burndowns (real-time), sprint
  demo recordings (bi-weekly to investors), monthly progress
  reports, quarterly KPI reviews
- **Quality Gates** — PR → lint → tests → review → staging → E2E
  → production; stricter pipeline for smart contract changes
- **Bug Severity SLAs** — P0: 1hr response / 4hr resolution;
  P1: 4hr / 24hr; P2: 1 day / 1 sprint; P3: 1 week / backlog
- **Definition of Done** — 8-point checklist including TypeScript
  strict mode, 80% test coverage, cross-browser testing, a11y,
  staging deploy, PO acceptance
